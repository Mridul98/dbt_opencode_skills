# Guidance (hudan) is a road with conditions — not a possession
**UID:** 202610122000
**Type:** Permanent / Domain Knowledge
**Source:** observable corpus facts in the cited `[s:a]` lines; classical distinction rahman/rahim and the "Fatihah answered by Baqarah" reading per consensus of tafsir-type analysis (Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi on 1:5–7 / 2:2–5); the "road not possession" synthesis is agent inference — verify.

## Concept
The Quran treats guidance (*huda/hidayah*) as its central verb, not a static label. Five properties recur across the text:

1. **Guidance is addressed, not owned.** It is "a guide" (*hudan*) — an object given direction. The Book is "a guide for the righteous" (2:2), and being "upon guidance" is a state one is *carried on* (2:5 "These are upon guidance from their Lord") — a preposition of dependence, not achievement.
2. **Guidance is conditional on stance, not lineage or liturgy.** The five traits of 2:3–4 (belief in the unseen, prayer, charity, affirmation of all revelation, certainty in the Hereafter) are the stated conditions — none is ancestry or mere identity claim.
3. **Guidance is a road (sirat) with traffic on it.** The Fatihah asks to be taken to "the straight path," and immediately describes it by its travelers: the blessed, the objects of anger, the astray (1:6–7). Guidance is therefore historical and communal — a route already walked.
4. **Guidance can be lost.** 2:6–7's sealed hearts show a real possibility of guidance being withheld after exposure to it; 28:56 confirms it is God's gift, not the preacher's power ("You cannot guide whom you love, but Allah guides whom He wills").
5. **Guidance is answered by seeking.** The Fatihah's petition (1:6) is, structurally, what al-Baqarah's opening then answers — the first thing the longest surah does after the disconnected letters is describe the guided. The book thus frames reading itself as response to a request.

## Evidence in the Text
* `[1:6–7]` "Guide us to the straight path. The path of those You have blessed..." — the petition and its social definition.
* `[2:2–5]` — the Book as "a guide for the righteous," their five traits, and the result: "upon guidance... the successful."
* `[2:6–7]` — the seal on hearts: guidance withheld after persistent rejection; "a severe torment."
* `[6:153]` "This is My path, straight, so follow it. And do not follow the other paths, lest they divert you..." — the road image with a plural warning.
* `[3:101]` "Whoever cleaves to Allah has been guided to a straight path." — guidance as holding on, not arriving.
* `[16:9]` "It is for Allah to point out the paths, but some of them are flawed. Had He willed, He could have guided you all." — divine sovereignty plus genuine human divergence.
* `[28:56]` "You cannot guide whom you love, but Allah guides whom He wills..." — guidance as God's prerogative.
* `[42:52]` "We made it a light, with which We guide whomever We will of Our servants. You surely guide to a straight path." — the Book as the lamp; the Prophet as road-sign.
* `[31:3]` "A guide and a mercy for the righteous." — the pair *hudan + rahmah* attached to the Quran itself.

## Practical Reading
When a passage mentions "guidance," ask four things of it: **Who is addressed? What condition is attached? What can forfeit it? What road-image appears?** This converts the most-used word in the book into four retrievable searches: `rg "guide|guidance" 03-mushaf/`, `rg "hudan" 03-mushaf/`. Read the Fatihah as the question and al-Baqarah 1–7 as the answer before going anywhere else; almost every "guidance" claim downstream is a variation on that pair.
