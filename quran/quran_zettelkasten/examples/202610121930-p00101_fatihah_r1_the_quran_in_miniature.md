# The Quran in miniature: praise, surrender, and the petition for guidance (1:1–7)
**UID:** 202610121930
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 1:1–7 (Ruku 1 of 1 in al-Fatihah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-13

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[1:1]` – `[1:7]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Al-Fatihah stages the entire Quran's worldview in seven lines: God is praised by His names, acknowledged as Master of the Judgment Day, worshipped and asked for help — and the single daily petition is *guidance to the path*, the question the rest of the book answers.
* **Placement:** The Mushaf's opening ruku; the recited pivot of every unit of prayer. Its three movements (praise → surrender → petition) set the interpretive frame for everything that follows — notably answered at the opening of al-Baqarah.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[1:1]`** "In the name of Allah, the Gracious, the Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The opening of all things by God's name; two mercy-names front the book (and, in this edition's numbering, this is ayah 1 — see Fleeting note below).
* **`[1:2]`** "Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds."
  * **Gloss:** All gratitude/praise belongs to Allah alone; "Lord of the Worlds" (rabb) states nurture and ownership of every realm.
* **`[1:3]`** "The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The mercy-names restated immediately after lordship — sovereignty is qualified by grace before judgment is mentioned.
* **`[1:4]`** "Master of the Day of Judgment."
  * **Gloss:** The one Day the reader will face alone; the pivot from praise to accountability.
* **`[1:5]`** "It is You we worship, and upon You we call for help."
  * **Gloss:** The worshipper's answer: exclusive devotion (ibadah) and exclusive reliance — the practical heart of tawhid.
* **`[1:6]`** "Guide us to the straight path."
  * **Gloss:** The sole petition of the surah: guidance (hidayah) to a "straight path" (sirat) — a road, not an abstraction.
* **`[1:7]`** "The path of those You have blessed, not of those against whom there is anger, nor of those who are misguided."
  * **Gloss:** The path is defined socially and historically: it has people on it — the blessed, and two counterexamples (anger and going astray). Guidance is a road already walked.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Allah`**: the proper name of the Creator (isim al-jalalah).
  * **Rendering:** edition A of this translation retains "Allah" (edition B renders "God").
  * **Elsewhere:** used in the very first numbered verse of the longest surah too — `[2:1]`'s letters give way to `[2:2]` "This is the Book..."; the name frames the whole corpus.
* **`ar-Rahman / ar-Rahim`**: rendered "the Gracious, the Merciful" — general mercy to creation and particular, sustained mercy to believers (classical distinction; see Source below).
  * **Rendering:** always "the Gracious, the Merciful" in the basmala lines.
  * **Elsewhere:** the basmala's unnumbered appearance before almost every surah header is a corpus-wide feature of this edition (e.g., the header of s027; 27:30 quotes it inside Solomon's letter).
* **`sirat` (path)**: a road; used with *mustaqim* ("straight") only here and in a handful of places.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:153]` "This is My path, straight, so follow it. And do not follow the other paths..." — the petition of 1:6 given its programmatic answer; `[3:101]` cleaving to Allah is being "guided to a straight path."
* **`yawm al-din`**: rendered "the Day of Judgment"; *din* bundles reckoning, recompense, and the debt of worship.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[1:4]` here; the day recurring across the book (e.g., `[2:4]` "certain of the Hereafter").

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Within-corpus pair:** `[2:1–7]` — al-Baqarah opens by describing *the guided* (2:5 "These are upon guidance from their Lord") and *the two failed categories* (2:6–7 the sealed disbelievers, then 2:8+ the hypocrites), answering Fatihah's petition verse-by-verse. (Source: structural observation on Itani's renderings; consistent with classical unity-of-Quran analysis.)
* **Parallel:** `[6:153]` — "My path, straight" vs "the other paths" defines what 1:6 asks for.
* **Parallel:** `[16:9]` — "It is for Allah to point out the paths, but some of them are flawed. Had He willed, He could have guided you all" — guidance as God's address and human risk.
* **Within-corpus:** `[27:30]` — Solomon's letter begins with the basmala, the one place the phrase appears inside a numbered verse's quoted text.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan, among the very first revealed (classical consensus). It is called *Umm al-Kitab* / "the Opening of the Book" in transmitted reports; seven oft-repeated verses (Source: consensus of classical tafsir, e.g., reports collected by Ibn Kathir on 1:1–7).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** none specific; the surah is liturgical, revealed for every unit of prayer (Source: consensus of classical tafsir).
